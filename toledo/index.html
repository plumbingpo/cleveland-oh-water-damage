Common Water Damage Issues in Toledo

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Toledo owners.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Toledo, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. When the clog becomes complete, every flush and every load of laundry comes back out downstairs. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. The team at Cleveland Restoration Co can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

How Drying Works on a Toledo Water Damage Job

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. On each Toledo job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most Toledo homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.
