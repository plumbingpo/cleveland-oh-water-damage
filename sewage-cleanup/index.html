Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in Cleveland, Cleveland Restoration Co is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

Safety Assessment

We start by measuring how far the sewage contamination reaches, noting any safety hazards and setting up containment so clean areas are not cross-contaminated.

Commercial drying equipment removes all residual moisture. Sewage odors are eliminated through industrial deodorizing. Clearance testing confirms the space is safe for occupancy.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
